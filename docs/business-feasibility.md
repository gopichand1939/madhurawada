# Madhurawada home kitchen — investment review

Research date: **10 September 2026**. Reference: a 2BHK , Madhurawada, Visakhapatnam. Operator: one homemaker. Investment and cooking experience not yet established.

## Executive verdict

**MAYBE — Test for 30 days first. Confidence: low.**

If this were my money and my sister's time, I would fund a small, permission-cleared, prepaid meal experiment. I would not fund an all-day Swiggy/Zomato kitchen, renovate the apartment, buy a commercial equipment package or hire a developer for a large platform before repeat demand is measured.

Five reasons:

1. Local restaurant supply is visible, but paid demand for this particular kitchen is unmeasured.
2. A homemaker is not free labour. The base model at 30 orders/day approximately breaks even after valuing her work and including a helper.
3. Direct delivery is not free. Low-value single meals delivered individually can have worse economics than expected.
4. The apartment may be commercially unsuitable even if recipes sell. Premises permission is a hard gate.
5. A batch pre-order model can reduce menu complexity and test repeat purchasing without committing to an all-day operation.

**Biggest opportunity:** dependable, limited-menu lunches for a small number of nearby apartment groups and working residents. **Biggest risk:** accepting too many time-sensitive orders before the operator and premises are ready.

**Proposed test:** one lunch window, three mains, pre-order cutoff, 2 km road-distance catchment, clustered deliveries, 10–15 paid orders/day initially. Test ₹150–₹220 single meals and ₹350–₹450 two-person combinations; delivery charged separately where clustering does not cover it. These are test prices, not verified local willingness to pay.

**Meaningful profit:** under the base assumptions, 50 orders/day produces about ₹12,464/month after owner pay; 75 produces ₹24,946. Those volumes should not be treated as suitable for an uninspected 2BHK. At 30/day, the base result is approximately **−₹922/month** after ₹20,000 owner labour and ₹8,000 helper labour. A strong direct-order model can do materially better; it must be demonstrated.

**Risk ceiling:** provisional ₹20,000 of unrecoverable operating loss over 30 days, excluding refundable deposits. Spend up to ₹10,000 in week one and release the rest only if paid demand and unit contribution pass. This is an experiment budget, not a statement that your household can safely afford it; reduce it if it consumes emergency savings or requires borrowing.

## 5. Unit economics, break-even and owner value

Full calculations for all 18 volume/scenario combinations and detailed monthly cost lines are in **financial-model.md**. Reproduce with **financial-model.py**; machine-readable results are in **financial-results.json**.

Definitions: ingredient gross profit = menu revenue − ingredients. Contribution = menu revenue − all variable costs. Operating cash surplus excludes depreciation. Economic profit deducts fixed costs, owner labour and depreciation. Post-income-tax net profit cannot be calculated without the ownership/tax facts.

At ₹220 AOV, base direct contribution is **₹83.60**, platform **₹30.30**, and a 60% direct mix **₹62.28**. Ingredient gross margin is 65%, but mixed contribution margin is 28.3% before fixed costs. A direct single-drop delivery cost ₹30 higher than assumed removes ₹30 from direct contribution. Website orders are not necessarily cheaper unless delivery and acquisition remain controlled.

At 30/day: monthly revenue ₹171,600; contribution ₹48,578; fixed costs including helper and owner ₹49,500; economic result **−₹922**. Before owner labour: ₹19,078 for 208 hours, roughly **₹92/hour**. Counting this as a ₹19,078 business profit hides the labour cost.

Break-even at that staffing tier: fixed costs/contribution = 49,500/62.28 = 795 orders/month, approximately **31/day**. Revenue at break-even ≈₹174,855. Required contribution at 780 orders =₹63.46. With fixed per-order costs ₹37.60 and variable percentage leaving 45.4%, break-even AOV=(63.46+37.60)/0.454 ≈**₹222.60**. But crossing the model's 30/day tier triggers extra premises and helper costs; at the 50/day tier fixed costs are ₹68,500 and break-even is about **43/day**, ₹241,969 revenue. There is no one break-even point independent of capacity.

To make ₹20,000/month above the owner allocation at the 50/day cost tier, contribution must reach ₹68.08/order. At the modelled channel economics that requires about **70.9% direct orders**, holding all other costs fixed. A direct-share target is useful only if acquisition and delivery costs stay within the model.

Worst-case conservative 10/day: revenue ₹46,800, contribution approximately ₹94/month, economic loss **₹41,406/month**. Cash loss excluding unpaid owner labour and depreciation is about **₹19,406/month**. Near-zero contribution cannot be fixed by buying more orders. Stop early if paid full-price orders are contribution-negative for a week.

Base 50/day: approximately ₹12,464 economic profit; ₹1 lakh investment/₹12,464 ≈8 steady-state profitable months. This is not a calendar payback forecast: allow ramp-up, losses and capital required for the commercial step. Conservative case has no payback. Optimistic 30/day: about ₹35,988/month; ₹1 lakh/that ≈2.8 steady-state months, but strong direct demand and cost control are unproven. Do not budget on optimistic recovery.

For a ₹200 menu order, ₹129 remains after those first two deductions; after ingredients, packaging, wastage/refund reserve, variable labour and acquisition, modelled contribution is only **₹25**. ## 8. Differentiation and acquisition

Ranked hypotheses: (1) reliable lunch slot with a rotating but small menu, measured portions and a pre-order cutoff; (2) apartment batch delivery with transparent delivered prices; (3) paid weekly meal plan after repeat proof; (4) pre-ordered family dinner packs; (5) specific regional specialty the operator demonstrably cooks better. 'Homemade', 'fresh' and 'affordable' are claims anyone can make. The service becomes harder to copy through reliable fulfilment, customer knowledge and dense routes, not slogans.

Only sell after applicable food/premises permissions are satisfied. Before then run interviews, price-choice tests and refundable reservations without producing food commercially.

| Day 30: economic close | All costs including owner + unpaid work | Positive economics at demonstrated volume or credible measured route to it | Persistent negative contribution; loss cap hit | Shut experiment down |

Do not interpret a 20-person pilot as statistical certainty. Record exposure denominator, price, paid amount, order source, dish, ingredient cost, packaging, delivery, time and repeat date. Separate dissatisfied customers from people who never wanted the product. No incentives for positive reviews.

**Three highest-priority experiments:** (1) 20–30 non-family households pay actual proposed prices; (2) fourteen-day repeat purchase, with no heavy discount, reaches ≥30%; (3) a ten-to-twenty-order lunch run proves safe cooking, timely clustered delivery and ≥₹60 contribution without exhausting the operator. Premises clearance precedes all three paid tests.
